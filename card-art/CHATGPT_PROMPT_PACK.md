# Brinewake: ChatGPT card art prompt pack

**How to use this file.** Paste the *Style brief* below into a new ChatGPT chat first, one chat per faction. Then, for each card, attach the reference render named under the card (if any) and paste the card's block. ChatGPT keeps a faction's look steadier inside one chat.

Contents: 8 factions, 232 cards (6 factions are ready with reference renders: 174 cards; Overcurrent and Gyre: 58 cards, no renders yet). Reference renders are in `card-art-reference-renders.zip`, in `renders/<faction>/`.

---

## STYLE BRIEF (paste this first)

You are painting card art for **Brinewake**, an original fantasy tactics card game. I'll send one card at a time. Every card is painted to the rules below. Please confirm, then wait for the first card.

**Setting.** Brinewake is a drained seabed beneath a sea held up in the sky by lighthouses. Bleak, strange, salt-stained, warm at human scale: white salt flats, black trench shadow, rust, bone, bioluminescence, and lighthouse beams in a subtly wrong-coloured sky. Nothing is clean or heroic-shiny; everything is weathered by salt and wet.

**Look.** Hand-painted digital fantasy card illustration in the tradition of classic collectible card games. Painterly brushwork, semi-realistic proportions, strong value contrast, one clear light source with a rim light, atmospheric depth behind the subject. Not a 3D render, not anime, not glossy concept-art polish.

**Palette.** Each faction has its own palette (hex codes are given at the top of that faction's section). Use those colours as the dominant scheme for every card of that faction, inside the muted, salt-stained world palette.

**Framing.** Vertical 2:3 portrait, 1024x1536, full-bleed painting to every edge. The card's name plate and rules panel cover the **bottom 40%**, so keep faces, hands and key props in the **top 60%**; the lower part can be legs, ground and shadow.

**Never include.** Text, letters, numbers, runes that read as text, logos, borders, frames, card UI, or watermarks.

**Matching the reference render.** For champions and heroes I attach the unit's in-game 3D model. Match it exactly: the same costume, colours, materials, props, headgear, body shape and silhouette. Do not add, remove or redesign gear. Paint it as a finished illustration, not as a 3D render, with more detail and texture than the model has. If the scene text and the render disagree about outfit, props or body, follow the render. For spells and equipment I attach the faction's unit lineup *only* for palette, materials and costume language; do not copy the lineup.

**Consistency rules.**
- Keep one painting style per faction chat. Once the first card of a faction looks right, match it on every later card ("match the painting style of the first card in this chat").
- Heroes are grander: a more dramatic pose and light, and a thin gold accent on the gear.
- Spells: the effect is the focal point; any figures are small or seen from behind, and faces aren't needed.
- Equipment: the object is shown large and close with a little world behind it, single-source lighting, no figure.
- Redo a card if it adds text, a border or a frame, or if the character doesn't match the render.

**Where finished art goes.** Save each result named by its card id (the code under the card name), e.g. `salt_concord_brineguard.png`. Put all of them in `card-art/finished/` (subfolders are fine; also mirrored at `/mnt/project-files/card-art/finished/`). Claude converts them to the game's 512x768 webp and they replace the placeholder art automatically.

**Reply with "ready" and I'll send the first card.**
